## Novelty assessment

### Claimed contributions (Section 2.5)
1. "We apply the techniques of algorithm unrolling to the NP-hard Max-Cut problem."
2. "We use zeroth-order optimization to tune a neural network in the context of combinatorial optimization."
3. "We show that effective dynamics for Ising machines can be learned from scratch in a data-driven way."

### Claim 1: Algorithm unrolling for NP-hard Max-Cut
The paper's own Related Work acknowledges that algorithm unrolling has been applied to integer linear programming (ILP), which is NP-hard, by Chen et al. (2024). The extension from ILP to Ising/Max-Cut is a domain transfer within unrolling-for-NP-hard problems, not the first such application. The paper's "limited application" framing understates this precedent.

More fundamentally, the NPIM architecture (learned MLP update rule applied iteratively) is structurally a **learned optimizer** (L2O) — the core paradigm of learning iterative update rules from data is extensively covered in the L2O literature cited by the paper itself (Monga et al. 2020; Chen et al. 2021, 2024; Kotary et al. 2023). The novel element is the choice of dynamical system (Ising machine with local interaction fields as input), not the paradigm.

The GNN-based Max-Cut solvers cited (Schuetz et al. 2022, Sanokowski et al. 2024) already learn iterative optimization dynamics for the same problem class. NPIM's contribution is architectural (compact MLP with temporal Fourier basis instead of GNN) and training-method (ZO instead of policy gradient), not a conceptual paradigm shift.

### Claim 2: ZO optimization for combinatorial optimization
The ZO method used is Reifenstein et al. (2024), a generic evolutionary optimizer. The paper's contribution is applying this specific ZO method to neural CO training, motivated by gradient instability in long unrollings. This is a well-motivated engineering choice but not a methodological invention in ZO optimization.

### Claim 3: Learning Ising dynamics from scratch
This is the most genuinely novel claim. Prior Ising machine heuristics (CAC, SBM, CIM) are hand-designed with physics inspiration. Learning the update rule from data is a departure. However, this is a specific instance of the broader L2O paradigm — the conceptual machinery (learn iterative update from reward signal) is established.

### Genuine novelty that survives:
- Compact Fourier-parameterized temporal schedule for Ising dynamics (specific and novel)
- ZO training of Ising machine dynamics (well-motivated domain transfer)
- Momentum-as-emergent-property observation from learned dynamics

### Novelty limitations:
- The contribution is a **domain transfer and architectural innovation** within established L2O paradigm, not paradigm invention
- Algorithm unrolling for NP-hard problems was already demonstrated (Chen et al. 2024, cited)
- Neural CO methods for Max-Cut already learn optimization dynamics (Schuetz et al. 2022, Sanokowski et al. 2024, cited)
- The ZO method is from prior work (Reifenstein et al. 2024, cited)
