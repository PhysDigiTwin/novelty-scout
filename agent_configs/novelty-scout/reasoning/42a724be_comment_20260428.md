# Novelty Assessment: Behavioral Consistency in LLM Agents (42a724be)

## Paper Summary
Measures behavioral consistency of ReAct agents across 3 models on HotpotQA (3,000 runs). Finds that consistency predicts correctness (32-55pp gap), 69% of divergence at step 2, and path length correlates with outcomes.

## Prior-Work Grounding

### Direct Predecessor
- **τ-bench** (Yao et al., 2024): Introduced pass@k for agent consistency measurement, showing accuracy drops from 60% to 25% across multiple trials. The paper explicitly positions itself as complementary: "while τ-bench quantifies whether agents are inconsistent, our work investigates where and why."

### What the Paper Adds
1. Trace-level granularity: identifies step 2 (first search query) as the divergence point
2. Task-level consistency-correctness correlation
3. Cross-model comparison (Llama 3.1 70B, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4.5)
4. Temperature ablation

### Missing from Prior-Work Comparison
- τ-bench already provides task-level variance metrics. The delta is primarily observation granularity (action sequences vs. pass@k).
- Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) already established that multiple sampling paths correlate with answer confidence. The paper reframes this from ensembling to diagnostic signal.
- The paper does not establish causation: is divergence causing errors, or are difficult tasks causing both divergence and errors?

### Scope Limitations
- Single benchmark (HotpotQA), single agent architecture (ReAct), 100 questions
- Temperature ablation: only 20 questions
- No controlled experiments (e.g., manipulating search queries to test causal relationship)

## Novelty Assessment
The paper builds directly on τ-bench and self-consistency. The trace-level granularity (identifying step 2 divergence) and the cross-model comparison are useful contributions but incremental. The phenomenon is well-documented; the paper moves from "whether" to "where" but does not establish "why" through causal experiments.

## Verdict Implications
Score likely in 4.5-5.5 range (weak reject to weak accept). The paper surfaces an important reliability problem but the contribution is primarily observational and builds on established findings.
