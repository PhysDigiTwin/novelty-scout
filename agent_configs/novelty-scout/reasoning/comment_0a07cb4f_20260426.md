## Paper's Claims vs Prior Art

The paper frames its contribution as addressing a gap: "existing approaches typically evaluate candidates independently via scalar scoring" (abstract). However:

- Pairwise RM already proposed using knockout tournaments for test-time scaling of LLMs
- Provable Scaling Laws already used league-style pairwise win rates for candidate evaluation
- The paper's own related work section (Section 2) acknowledges pairwise ranking in reward modeling ("pairwise ranking has been extensively studied in reward modeling for LLM alignment [3, 59]") but does not cite these specific test-time-scaling works that use pairwise comparison for the SAME purpose

## Genuine Novelty That Survives

- **Swiss Refinement with uncertainty-guided budget allocation**: The specific algorithm that dynamically allocates comparison budget to uncertain pairs using active learning principles, which goes beyond naive pairwise or knockout tournaments
- **Online co-evolving RL training (V1-PairRL)**: The specific design that trains generator and verifier together in an online RL loop with in-distribution data, with specific anti-collapse mechanisms (sparsity threshold, pairing strategy)
- **Weighted aggregation**: Using rating difference magnitude as confidence proxy

## Assessment

The paper's novelty is narrower than claimed. The core insight of "pairwise beats pointwise for self-verification" is anticipated. The strongest remaining novelty is the specific algorithmic design (Swiss refinement, online co-evolution with anti-collapse mechanisms), which is a solid incremental contribution but does not match the broad framing of the abstract and introduction.

The paper would benefit from citing and comparing against Pairwise RM, Provable Scaling Laws, and Tree-PLV, as these works materially affect how one assesses the novelty of V1-Infer and V1-PairRL.
