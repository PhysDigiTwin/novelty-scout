# Novelty Audit: Sign Lock-In (0ce14447)

## Paper Summary
"Sign Lock-In: Randomly Initialized Weight Signs Persist and Bottleneck Sub-Bit Model Compression" claims three contributions:
1. Empirical discovery that weight signs persist from initialization and resist compression
2. Sign lock-in theory: stopping-time analysis proving geometric tail on sign flips
3. Lock-in enhancement: gap-based initialization + outward-drift regularizer for sub-bit compression

## Prior-Work Scout Results
The prior-work scout (Gemini Pro) identified four prior works the paper should have cited:

1. **Gadhikar & Burkholz (2024)** - "Masks, Signs, and Learning Rate Rewinding" (ICLR): Empirically studied that signs are crucial for Lottery Tickets and sparse training, and that standard pruning fails because it cannot correct bad initial signs. This directly overlaps with the paper's "empirical discovery" of sign persistence.

2. **Gadhikar et al. (2024)** - "Sign-In to the Lottery: Reparameterizing Sparse Training From Scratch" (arXiv): Investigates how initialization signs dictate sparse training success and proposes reparameterization to enable sign flips. This is the most direct prior art: it already identified that random initial signs are difficult to flip with SGD.

3. **BTC-LLM (2024)**: A framework for sub-1-bit LLM quantization (0.7-1.1 bits) using binary codebooks. This challenges the paper's claim of an "insurmountable one-bit wall."

4. **OneBit (2024)**: 1-bit model compression using matrix-decomposition initialization. The paper's "compressible sign template" (low-rank template via GH^T) conceptually overlaps with OneBit's approach.

## Analysis

### What is genuinely novel
The stopping-time analysis (Theorem 3.6) providing a formal geometric-tail bound on sign flips under SGD noise is the paper's core contribution. Prior work observed sign persistence empirically; this paper provides a mechanistic explanation. The gap-based initialization and outward-drift regularizer derived from the theory are principled interventions.

### What is overclaimed
1. **"Empirical discovery" (contribution #1)**: Gadhikar et al. (2024) already empirically characterized sign persistence in sparse training. The paper's claim to have *discovered* that signs persist from initialization ignores this prior work. The phenomenon is not newly discovered; it is newly formalized.

2. **"One-bit wall" framing**: BTC-LLM already achieves sub-1-bit compression. The existence of working sub-bit methods undercuts the claim that sign persistence creates an insurmountable barrier. The paper should frame its contribution as a *formal explanation* of why sign compression is hard, not as the *discovery* that it is hard.

3. **Related work thinness**: The related work section discusses compression methods broadly but does not engage with the specific works (Gadhikar et al.) that studied sign dynamics. Appendix B may contain additional discussion, but the main body should address these directly given how central sign persistence is to the paper's narrative.

## Verdict Impact
The formal theory is a solid ICML contribution. However, the overclaiming of empirical novelty and missing citations to directly relevant prior work pull the novelty score down. The paper should recalibrate: the contribution is the *theory* explaining a *known* phenomenon, not the discovery of the phenomenon itself.
