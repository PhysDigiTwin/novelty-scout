### Key Prior Works Not Adequately Positioned

1. **Li et al. (2024/2026) — "Online LLM watermark detection via e-processes" / "Online Detection via E-Processes"**: Directly tackles the same problem of anytime-valid inference in LLM watermarking using e-values/e-processes for early stopping without inflating false positive rates. 2. **Kim et al. (March 2026) — "Watermarking System: Theoretical Analysis and Empirical Validation"**: Proposes an optimal stopping framework for LLM watermark detection, sharing the high-level goal of moving from fixed-sample verification to sequential testing to reduce token budgets.

3. **Huang et al. (2023) — "Towards Optimal Statistical Watermarking"**: Foundational work on optimal watermarking as hypothesis testing. The paper cites this but must differentiate its anytime-valid approach from this fixed-horizon baseline.

### Overlap Assessment

"There is a substantial conceptual overlap regarding the use of sequential hypothesis testing and e-values (or e-processes) to achieve anytime-valid watermark detection. Both the submission and works like Li et al. recognize that fixed-horizon testing is suboptimal for streaming LLM generation and use e-values to enable valid early stopping."

### What Remains Genuinely Novel

The specific "Anchored" formulation: using an anchor distribution to approximate the target model, providing a rigorous closed-form solution for the optimal e-value with respect to worst-case log-growth rate (Theorem 4.1). The theoretical characterization of the optimal coupling combined with exact expected stopping time bounds is a substantive mathematical contribution beyond off-the-shelf e-process application.

### Novelty Risk Assessment

"The most significant novelty risk is the submission's claim to be the 'first e-value-based watermarking framework.' Search grounding reveals that the application of e-processes to LLM watermark detection has been explored concurrently or prior. The submission fails to benchmark against these other sequential/stopping frameworks."
