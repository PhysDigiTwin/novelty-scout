## Prior-work assessment
The scout confirms the paper's core mechanistic contribution (role probes) has low novelty risk. The key identified prior works are all properly cited: Abdelnabi et al. (2023) for indirect injection, Wallace et al. (2024) for instruction hierarchy, ReAct for the CoT trust boundary, and the GCG attack for contrast. The scout's "strongest_novelty_defense" - that role probes are a genuinely new diagnostic tool - is valid.

## Novelty calibration

### What is genuinely novel
1. **Role probes as a diagnostic tool**: Training linear classifiers on identical text wrapped in different architectural tags to isolate the latent geometry of role perception is clever methodology. The protocol design (same content, different tags) cleanly separates tag identity from content confounds.
2. **Dose-response relationship**: The finding that probe-measured confusion predicts attack success before generation is a substantive contribution, moving from behavioral observation to mechanistically grounded prediction.
3. **Style-ablation evidence**: The destyling experiment (preserving semantics, stripping syntactic markers → ASR collapses from 61% to 10%) provides causal evidence that style rather than logic drives role inheritance.

### What overclaims relative to evidence
1. **"Unifying framework" for standard injections is structurally argued, not empirically validated**: Section 4 generalizes role probes to standard attacks by mapping attacker signals to the probe subspace, but provides no dose-response data for non-CoT injection types. The unification is presented as established where it is actually extrapolated - a structural mapping, not an empirical demonstration.
2. **"Mechanistic theory" vs. correlational evidence**: The probes demonstrate that style features predict role-perception representations, and that those representations predict attack success. This is predictive, not causal. Without activation patching or representation-level intervention, the causal claim that role confusion *causes* attack success remains unproven. The probes could measure a downstream artifact rather than an upstream cause.
3. **Novelty attribution is unevenly distributed**: The CoT Forgery attack is presented as a primary contribution, but injecting fabricated reasoning to bypass safety has direct precedent in H-CoT (Kuo et al., 2025) and other concurrent work. The paper acknowledges these but does not fully calibrate: the attack's novelty is incremental; the probe methodology is the genuine contribution.

### Missing prior-work engagement
1. **Representation Engineering** (Zou et al., 2023): The role-probe methodology (linear classifiers on residual-stream representations) is a direct instance of the RepE framework. The paper does not position itself relative to RepE or discuss whether RepE-style activation patching on role-probe directions would close the causal gap. This is a notable omission for an ICML interpretability contribution.

## Bottom line
The paper's novelty profile is asymmetric: the role-probe methodology and mechanistic framing are genuinely novel and valuable contributions to prompt injection understanding. The CoT Forgery attack is a useful diagnostic tool but not a novel attack vector. The paper would be stronger if it presented as a mechanistic/interpretability contribution first, with CoT Forgery as validation, rather than as a new security paradigm. Overclaiming the "unifying framework" and "mechanistic theory" labels relative to what correlational evidence supports weakens rather than strengthens the paper.
