## Novelty Assessment

### Genuinely Novel
1. **Iterative tool use for medical verification in an RL loop** is a clear step beyond Med-PRM's static RAG. The ability to search, evaluate, and re-search adaptively during verification is genuinely differentiated.
2. **Trace-level supervision** (requiring only correctness labels, not step-level expert annotations) is a practical contribution that reduces annotation burden compared to PRM approaches.
3. **8x sampling efficiency** is a substantial practical gain.

### Novelty Limitations
1. **Tool-augmented RL is established in general domains.** ReAct (Yao et al., 2023), Toolformer (Schick et al., 2024), TIR-Judge, and Themis have all explored tool-augmented reasoning and verification. Med-TIV is a domain application of these ideas to medicine, not a new paradigm.
2. **Med-PRM already does RAG-based medical verification.** The core insight — that external knowledge grounding improves medical verification — was established by Med-PRM. Med-TIV's contribution is iterative (multi-turn) retrieval within an RL curriculum, which is a refinement, not a discovery.
3. **The curriculum mechanism is standard.** The adaptive curriculum (difficulty-based training data distribution) is a well-known RL training technique, not a novel contribution.
4. **Missing general-domain tool-verification references.** The prior-work scout identifies TIR-Judge and Themis as general-domain tool-integrated verification methods that should contextualize Med-TIV's contribution. Without these, the paper appears to claim more novelty than warranted.

## Overall Judgment

Med-TIV is a well-engineered domain application with strong empirical results. The iterative tool use + RL curriculum combination is a practical advance over Med-PRM's static RAG, and the 8x sampling efficiency is genuinely valuable. However, the core ideas (tool-augmented RL, retrieval-augmented verification, curriculum learning) are all established in prior work, and the contribution is the specific combination and medical-domain validation. The paper would be stronger with comparisons to general-domain tool-verification methods (TIR-Judge, Themis).

Score range: Weak accept (5.0-6.0). The engineering is solid and the domain application is valuable, but the conceptual novelty is incremental.
