## Paper Claims

The paper claims three contributions:
1. Identifying epistemic uncertainty of PRMs on OOD traces as critical for inference-time search
2. Theoretical proof that uncertainty-aware (UCB-style) search achieves sublinear regret vs. linear for standard search
3. UATS: MC Dropout estimation + heuristic search + RL-based adaptive controller (A-UATS)

## Novelty Assessment

### Genuinely Novel Elements

1. **Systematic empirical characterization of PRM epistemic uncertainty on OOD traces (Section 4.1):** The measurement of PRM score variance across PRM-policy model combinations (Math-Shepherd vs Qwen2.5-Math; LLemma-7B vs Qwen2.5-Instruct-7B) provides a direct empirical quantification of the OOD uncertainty problem. While Kotelevskii et al. (2024, cited) noted epistemic uncertainty in PRMs, I am not aware of prior work that systematically measures and characterizes PRM score variance across distribution shifts at this level of detail.

2. **RL-based adaptive controller for dynamic search budget allocation (Section 5.3):** The formulation of search hyperparameter modulation as an MDP with a learnable controller that takes PRM score/uncertainty statistics as state is a legitimate engineering contribution. This goes beyond static heuristics.

### Novelty-Weak Elements

1. **MC Dropout for epistemic uncertainty (Section 5.1):** This is directly from Gal & Ghahramani (2016). The paper's contribution is applying this established technique to PRM-guided tree search, which is a domain transfer, not a methodological invention. The paper acknowledges this lineage.

2. **UCB-style selection (Eq. 8):** The upper confidence bound formulation (mean + bonus term) is standard from the bandit literature. The paper's Proposition 4.2 is a straightforward application of UCB regret analysis to the PRM search setting.

3. **Prior work on PRM uncertainty:** Kotelevskii et al. (2024) already identified epistemic uncertainty in PRMs. The paper's incremental contribution is the systematic empirical characterization and algorithmic integration.

### Critical Novelty Limitations

1. **Unbiasedness Assumption Contradicts Problem Statement (Proposition 4.2):** The paper's core empirical claim is that PRMs are systematically overconfident on OOD data -- a form of bias. Yet Proposition 4.2 explicitly assumes unbiased estimators (E_φ[R̄_t(h)] = R*(h)). If the PRM is biased on OOD (the very regime the paper studies), the regret guarantee does not apply. The theoretical contribution does not actually support the paper's claimed regime of operation.

2. **Theorem-Implementation Gap:** Proposition 4.2 requires K_t = Ω(t) for sublinear regret -- that is, the number of MC Dropout samples must grow with time. The implementation uses fixed K_0 = 7. The theoretical justification does not apply to the actual algorithm. This is not a minor mismatch; it means the paper's strongest novelty claim (theoretical guarantee) does not characterize the method being evaluated.

3. **Missing Baseline:** ReST-MCTS* (a recent PRM-guided MCTS approach for LLM reasoning) should have been included as a baseline. The paper compares against BoN, self-consistency, beam search, and several tree-search variants, but omits ReST-MCTS* which is directly relevant to the tree-search setting and could absorb some of the reported gains.

4. **Component Novelty Disaggregation:** The paper does not ablate the contribution of individual components to novelty. MC Dropout estimation is from Gal & Ghahramani 2016. UCB selection is standard bandit methodology. The heuristic budget allocation is a straightforward application. The RL controller is the most novel piece but its marginal contribution vs. the heuristic is not isolated.

### Related Work Framing

The Related Work section covers inference-time reasoning, PRMs, and uncertainty estimation. However:
- It does not cite ReST-MCTS* or other recent MCTS-based reasoning work
- The connection to the broader epistemic uncertainty literature (beyond MC Dropout) is thin
- The framing of "novel identification of PRM epistemic uncertainty" overstates the contribution given Kotelevskii et al. 2024

## Conclusion

The paper addresses a real problem (PRM miscalibration under distribution shift) and the empirical characterization of this phenomenon is valuable. However, the claimed theoretical contribution is undermined by an assumption that contradicts the paper's own problem motivation, and the gap between theorem requirements and implementation means the theory does not characterize the actual algorithm. The algorithmic components are individually well-established (MC Dropout, UCB), and the novelty is primarily in their combination and the RL controller. The paper would be strengthened by resolving the unbiasedness paradox, demonstrating that the algorithm's behavior matches the theory, and including ReST-MCTS* as a baseline.

**Novelty Rating:** Moderate. The problem framing and empirical characterization are the strongest contributions. The theoretical guarantee does not apply to the implemented algorithm. The algorithmic contribution is a well-engineered combination of established techniques with one novel element (RL controller).
