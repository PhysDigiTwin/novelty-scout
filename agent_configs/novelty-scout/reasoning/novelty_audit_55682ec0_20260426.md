## Paper Summary

The paper proposes a 4-dimension reliability framework for AI agents (consistency, robustness, predictability, safety) adapted from safety-critical engineering (aviation, nuclear power, automotive, railways). It defines 12 concrete metrics and evaluates 14 models across GAIA and τ-bench benchmarks. The central empirical claim: reliability gains (R ≈ 0.03/yr) lag far behind accuracy gains (≈ 0.21/yr) over 18 months of model releases.

## Novelty Assessment

### 1. Framework novelty: Genuine synthesis, moderate conceptual depth

The cross-domain translation from safety-critical engineering to AI agent evaluation is the paper's primary intellectual contribution. The four dimensions (consistency, robustness, predictability, safety) are well-established in dependability engineering (Avizienis et al. 2004, IEC 61508, FAA AC 25.1309). The paper's contribution is mapping them onto computable, LLM-agent-specific metrics.

**Closest prior work:**
- **Liang et al. (HELM, cited [37]):** Provides holistic multi-metric evaluation for language models, but targets LMs rather than tool-using agents and does not organize metrics around safety-critical engineering principles.
- **Kapoor et al. (HAL, cited [31]):** Same-author work providing agent evaluation infrastructure. The current paper builds on HAL but adds the reliability decomposition explicitly.
- **Avizienis et al. (2004, cited [4]):** The foundational dependability taxonomy (availability, reliability, safety, integrity, maintainability). The paper acknowledges this lineage but operationalizes it specifically for LLM agents.

**Assessment:** The framework is a genuine synthesis. It is not an invention of new reliability concepts but rather a careful translation of established engineering principles into a new domain. This is valuable applied methodology work, not foundational theory. The 4-dimension decomposition appears to be the authors' own mapping — the safety-critical engineering sources do not converge on these four as a canonical set.

### 2. Metric novelty: Adaptations, not inventions

Of the 12 metrics, almost all are standard statistical or ML evaluation measures applied to the agent domain:

| Metric | Underlying Concept | Novel? |
|--------|-------------------|--------|
| Cout (outcome consistency) | Normalized Bernoulli variance | Standard binomial variance normalization |
| Cd_traj (trajectory distribution) | Jensen-Shannon divergence of action distributions | Standard divergence applied to agent traces |
| Cs_traj (trajectory sequence) | Levenshtein distance | Standard edit distance |
| Cres (resource consistency) | Coefficient of variation | Standard CV with exponential transform |
| Rfault/Renv/Rprompt | Accuracy ratio under perturbation | Standard perturbation testing |
| Pcal | Expected Calibration Error (ECE) | Standard, from Guo et al. 2017 [21] |
| PAUROC | AUC-ROC | Standard discrimination metric |
| Pbrier | Brier score | Standard proper scoring rule |
| Scomp | Violation rate | Standard compliance check |
| Sharm | Weighted harm severity | Simple heuristic weighting |

**Assessment:** The contribution is in the curation and organization, not in the metrics themselves. This is defensible — the paper explicitly frames itself as a measurement framework, not a methods paper. However, the paper's framing could be more explicit that it is adapting known measures rather than creating new ones.

### 3. Missing citations and prior-work gaps

**Mehta (2026), "When Agents Disagree With Themselves"** — flagged by Factual Reviewer. This is a significant omission for the consistency dimension. Mehta directly studies repeated-run behavioral consistency in ReAct-style agents, measures unique action-sequence diversity and first-divergence points, and finds consistency predicts correctness. The paper's consistency metrics (Cout, Cd_traj, Cs_traj) overlap substantively. The paper should cite Mehta and clarify differentiation.

**Dependability frameworks for ML:** The paper cites Avizienis et al. (2004) and Laprie for foundational dependability concepts but may under-credit more recent work applying dependability concepts to ML systems. I note but do not flag as critical — the paper's contribution is sufficiently specific to agents that broad ML-dependability overviews would not weaken it.

**Agent safety/security benchmarks:** The paper cites AgentHarm [2] and Foerster et al. [19] for safety/security. It could strengthen positioning relative to work on agent-specific failure taxonomies (e.g., SABER [10], which the paper already uses for dataset curation).

### 4. Empirical novelty

The 14-model evaluation with repeated runs, perturbation protocols, and safety adjudication is a substantial empirical contribution. The finding that reliability lags accuracy is not surprising (it follows from known non-determinism and sensitivity issues), but the quantitative characterization across three providers and two benchmarks is novel in scope.

### 5. Fair positioning assessment

The paper positions itself fairly relative to:
- HELM (Liang et al.): acknowledges as a multi-metric inspiration but distinguishes agent focus
- HAL (Kapoor et al.): built on same infrastructure, properly cited
- Safety-critical engineering: properly credited as the source of the framework
- Calibration and selective prediction literature: properly cited

The paper could be clearer about which metrics are adapted from existing ML literature versus proposed de novo. Table 2 presents all 12 metrics as the paper's framework without clearly demarcating prior attribution for individual measures.

## Verdict Implications

This paper makes a **genuine but moderate** novelty contribution. The framework synthesis is real and useful; individual metrics are mostly adaptations of known measures. The empirical characterization is the strongest contribution for ICML, and the missing Mehta citation is a fixable scholarship gap rather than a fatal novelty defect. If the Mehta citation is added and metric provenance is clarified, the contribution is solidly in the weak-to-strong accept range for an applied methodology/benchmarking paper.
