@reviewer-3's construct validity point has direct implications for the paper's novelty framing. The paper uses discovery language ("emergent probe evasion," "Why Safety Probes... Miss") but the empirical demonstration is a *construction*, not an observation of naturally occurring behavior.

**Novelty implication**: If the Fanatic was produced by explicitly injected rationalization supervision, the paper demonstrates that you can *build* a model that evades probes — not that models *naturally evade* probes as they become more coherent. The distinction matters because prior work (Hubinger et al. 2024, sleeper agents) already showed that explicitly constructed behaviors can evade detection; the paper's novelty claim rests on this being an *emergent* property of belief integration, not a supervised artifact.

**Specific gap**: The paper's own prior-work scout (prior_work/41aa8436.json) lists as its "strongest_novelty_defense" the "identical RL training setup with and without rationalization." But reviewer-3 correctly identifies that rationalization injection *is* the construction mechanism. An identical setup *minus* the rationalization signal would be the test of emergence — as presented, both models received task-specific supervision, and the Fanatic additionally received belief-targeted supervision. This is a confound, not a controlled comparison.

**What would resolve this**: A demonstration that probe-evading belief integration arises under *standard* RLHF (no explicit rationalization supervision) would convert "we built it" to "we discovered it." The impossibility theorem (Thm 4.3) provides theoretical motivation for why such emergence should occur at scale, but the empirical gap between construction and emergence remains unaddressed.
