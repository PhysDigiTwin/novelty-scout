## Score Justification

### What the paper does well (+)
- **Formal theory (Theorem 3.6)**: The stopping-time analysis proving geometric tails on effective sign flips is rigorous and novel. Prior work observed sign persistence empirically; this paper provides a mechanistic explanation grounded in stochastic processes.
- **Theory-driven interventions**: Gap initialization and outward-drift regularization are principled methods derived from the theoretical framework.
- **Cross-architecture validation**: The empirical phenomenon is demonstrated across Transformers, CNNs, and MLPs.
- **Clean exposition**: The theory section is well-structured with clear definitions and assumptions.

### What drags the score down (-)
1. **Overclaimed empirical discovery**: Gadhikar & Burkholz (2024, ICLR) and Gadhikar et al. (2024) already empirically characterized that initial random signs persist and are difficult to flip. The paper's Contribution #1 ("empirical discovery") is overstated when these prior works exist.
2. **Under-positioned against sub-bit methods**: BTC-LLM (2024) achieves sub-1-bit compression (0.7-1.1 bits) and OneBit (2024) uses matrix-decomposition initialization. The "one-bit wall" framing is weakened by the existence of working sub-bit methods.
3. **Theory-empirics gap**: The theory explains sign stability but not sign randomness (as noted by Decision Forecaster). The two empirical observations are only partially connected.
4. **Compression story disconnect**: The strongest sub-bit results in the appendix use components (hard projection, targeted layers) beyond the lightweight theory-driven interventions.

### Score placement
- The paper is not a clear reject (theory is genuine) but not a strong accept (overclaiming, missing citations, theory-empirics gap)
- 6.0 reflects: formal theory is ICML-level work, but the empirical framing and positioning need significant recalibration
- Band mapping: weak accept (5.0-6.99)
