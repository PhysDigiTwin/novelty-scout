# Novelty Audit: Simple Baselines are Competitive with Code Evolution

**Paper ID:** 0bb9fe86-b711-4b1f-bec5-035ec976f497

## Paper Summary

The paper shows that two simple baselines (IID Random Sampling and Sequential Conditioned Sampling) match or exceed sophisticated code evolution methods across three domains: mathematical bounds discovery, agentic scaffold design, and ML competitions. The paper argues that search space design is the primary driver of performance, not the evolution algorithm.

## Prior Work Assessment

The paper omits engagement with two key prior works that anticipate its core findings:

1. **Sutton (2019), "The Bitter Lesson"** — the general principle that leveraging search/scale/computation beats hand-crafted methods. The paper's finding that simple search matches complex evolutionary pipelines is a direct instantiation of this principle.

2. **Chen et al. (2021), "Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code" (Codex paper)** — introduced pass@k, which is functionally equivalent to the IID RS baseline. The finding that IID sampling from an LLM produces competitive solutions was established here.

Additionally, the "representation matters more than search" insight has a long history in AI (Amarel, 1968).

## What's Genuinely Novel

1. The 20.5x quantification — that search space design improvements outweigh search algorithm improvements by ~20x — is a crisp empirical result
2. The "Small-N Selection Trap" identification for agentic scaffolds
3. The cross-domain scope (math bounds, agents, ML competitions) with controlled comparisons

## Verdict Implications

This is a valuable empirical audit paper but not a novel conceptual contribution. The core finding is predicted by established principles. A fair score would be in the weak accept range (5.0-6.0), with credit for the comprehensive empirical demonstration but pulling down for the anticipated core finding.
