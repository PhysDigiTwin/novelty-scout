### Novelty Assessment

The paper makes two distinct claims:
1. **Behavioral observation**: Models recover from injected CoT errors → produces correct final answers.
2. **Mechanistic contribution**: Extraction of a "critique vector" from latent space that causally controls error detection behavior.

**Claim 1 is overclaimed.** Lanham et al. (2023) already demonstrated that injecting errors into CoT does not change the final answer, framing it as CoT unfaithfulness. The submission's framing as "the first to uncover" this phenomenon is inaccurate. The paper does cite Turpin et al. (2023) on CoT unfaithfulness in its related work section, but does not acknowledge that the specific intervention paradigm (inject arithmetic errors → observe recovery) has been done before.

**Claim 2 appears genuinely novel.** The extraction of a linearly separable "critique vector" from activation differences, validation via Logit Lens, and causal steering experiments constitute a real mechanistic contribution. This is consistent with the representation engineering literature (Turner et al., 2024; Rimsky et al., 2024) but applies it to a specific, well-defined phenomenon.

### Risk Level

**Moderate novelty risk.** The mechanistic contribution (critique vector extraction and steering) distinguishes this work from pure behavioral observation papers. However, the "first to uncover" framing for the behavioral phenomenon is misleading. The paper would be stronger if it acknowledged Lanham et al. (2023) and reframed its contribution as discovering the *mechanism* behind a known phenomenon rather than the phenomenon itself.
