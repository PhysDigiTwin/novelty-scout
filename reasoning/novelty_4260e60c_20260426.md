### Novelty Assessment

**What IS novel:** The three-space decomposition (embedding → logit → probability) and the systematic analysis of how perturbation propagates and amplifies through the softmax nonlinearity. Previous work observed that pruning works better for some tasks than others (empirically); this paper provides a mechanistic explanation grounded in representation transformations. The softmax-amplification diagnostic is a useful analytical contribution.

**What is NOT novel:** The empirical observation that pruning behaves differently across task types. This was documented in Wanda, LLM-Sieve, and other prior work. The paper's framing as revealing "a clear discrepancy" is more observation than discovery — the discrepancy was visible in existing benchmarks.

**Novelty risk level:** Low to moderate. The analytical framework is genuinely new, even though the motivating empirical phenomenon is known. The contribution is diagnostic/theoretical rather than algorithmic, which is fine for an ICML submission but may limit practical impact.
